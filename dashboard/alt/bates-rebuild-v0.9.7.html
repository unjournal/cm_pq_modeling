5. The Learn page overstates the seed train

15 Nov 2026 · no model change

Finding. The Learn page puts the seed train at 5–10% of production cost, and a few lines later at 5–15%, "mostly labour". It cites Humbird's $1/kg labour figure, but that covers the whole plant's staff (95 people for fed-batch, 132 for perfusion) against a total cost of $37/kg, so all plant labour is about 3% of cost. The page also says seed-train medium volumes are orders of magnitude smaller than in production. In the model the seed train is equipment only, about 0.5% of cost, and that capital ratio is consistent with Humbird (about $2M of seed-train reactors against $34M of production reactors).

6. The Learn page's cost breakdown describes the cheap worlds

15 Nov 2026 · no model change

Finding. The cost breakdown summary says capital is 15–35% of total cost and fixed running costs 10–25%. In the model they are about 7% and 3.5% across all worlds, but about 13% and 22% in the cheapest 10% of worlds. Published cost studies model plants near parity with conventional meat, so their shares match the model's cheap worlds, not its typical one.

7. Food-grade reactors: a yes/no technology, not a blended share

16 Nov 2026 · v0.6

The original bought a blended share of reactor capacity (55% ± 15) at food-grade prices and the rest at pharma prices. This doesn't make sense at a frontier factory, and is more relevant as an industry average.

8. Choices removed: the plant takes the cheapest route the technology allows

16 Nov 2026 · v0.8

There's no point imagining a frontier plant which uses a worse process.

9. Error in the original: hydrolysate medium charged even when dearer

16 Nov 2026 · corrected in v0.8

Repeated issue with the model where technology which is cheaper on average is used even when simulated result makes it more expensive in some particular world.

10. Failed runs also gross up the harvest cost

16 Nov 2026 · no model change

Finding. The share of output lost to failed runs grosses up every cost line, including harvest. But a failed run is thrown away, not harvested, so harvest shouldn't scale with failures. Recovery losses in the harvest step itself do belong there.

11. Plant costs: food-grade reactors, the whole-plant multiplier and one operating cost

16 Nov 2026 · v0.8.6

Change. (1) Reactor prices are entered as two prices, pharma-grade and food-grade (default 0.35 × pharma-grade), drawn together so they move as one; this replaces the original's separate 0.15–0.55 ratio. (2) The whole-plant multiplier also has a food-grade column, since a food-grade plant may need far less cleanroom space, sterile pipework and validation (default: no difference). If food-grade reactors work, the plant uses whichever installed cost is lower. (3) Fixed running costs ($1–6/kg wet at a 20 kt reference plant, scaled by plant size and utilisation) and utilities and consumables ($0.30–3/kg wet) are merged into one plant operating cost per kg: central $23.50, 95% optimistic $9.00 per kg dry ($4.70 and $1.80 per kg wet), matching the median and 2.5th percentile of their sum. The fixed-cost scale exponent goes with them.

12. Error in the original: economies of scale never run out

16 Nov 2026 · corrected in v0.8.7

The original model has no limit to the scaling factor of capital, which means the best plant is just one which grows massive. This is not plausible, and leads to spurious effectiveness.

13. Corrections after a review by Claude

16 Nov 2026 · v0.9.4

Errors in the original model, corrected here.

  • Free medium recycling. The medium-use multiplier can be drawn below 1 (about a third of continuous-culture draws, and some for the other processes). Using less than one reactor-volume of medium per harvest requires recycling spent medium, which is never costed. The multiplier is now floored at 1. This removes about 10% of competitive worlds (often the nutrient floor already binds, so the multiplier doesn't matter).
  • Maturity biases every probability. The maturity nudge is centred on 0.5, but maturity averages 0.4, so every technology probability came out 2–3 points below the value entered, and the cost of capital about 0.3 points above it. The nudge is now centred on maturity's own average. Maturity then only links the technologies; its central value no longer moves anything.
  • Wet-basis costs made the answer depend on how wet the cells are. Operating costs and supplemental proteins were entered per kg of wet biomass and converted at a fixed 20% solids, so changing that fraction from 0.20 to 0.25 doubled the number of competitive worlds without any change in the world. All inputs are now per kg dry (defaults converted at 20%, so results are unchanged by this alone), and the solids fraction no longer affects the result.
  • Fed-batch downtime may be counted twice. The replacement time includes batch turnaround, and utilisation is described as covering changeovers too. Utilisation is now defined to exclude batch turnaround. Small, and fed-batch is almost never chosen in competitive worlds.
