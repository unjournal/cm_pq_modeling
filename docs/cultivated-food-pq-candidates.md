# Candidate Forecasting Questions: Cultivated & Precision-Fermentation Food Products

*Draft for discussion — potential Metaculus / forecasting tournament questions.*  
*Five questions spanning market penetration, consumer acceptance, and price parity for cultivated and precision-fermentation food products.*

---

## Common operationalization notes

**"Price parity" definition used throughout**: unless stated otherwise, "price parity" means the cultivated/PF product is available for commercial purchase at a price ≤ a stated multiple of the conventional commodity benchmark (defined per question), for orders of ≥ the stated minimum quantity.

**Resolution sources**: each question specifies acceptable verification sources. In general, company price sheets corroborated by independent industry reports (USDA, FAO, GIRA, Rabobank, Bloomberg) are preferred over press releases alone.

**"Commercially available"**: means available for ongoing regular purchase by businesses or consumers — not a one-time event, limited pilot, or laboratory demonstration. At minimum: product available for ≥ 3 consecutive months, purchasable by any qualifying buyer without a special relationship with the company.

## Question 1 — Cultivated chicken: meaningful blend in retail ground chicken

### Title
Will a commercial ground/minced chicken product containing ≥10% cultivated chicken cells (by raw weight) be available in mainstream retail in any G20 country by 2035?

### Background
Cultivated chicken — chicken muscle cells grown in bioreactors without slaughter — has received regulatory approval for limited commercial sale in Singapore (2020) and the United States (2023). Near-term commercial strategy for most companies involves blended products: conventional chicken combined with a cultivated fraction, reducing the quantity of conventionally produced chicken required while keeping costs manageable. A 10% cultivated fraction is a meaningful threshold — it represents a commercially viable blend at scale rather than a trace ingredient, and would begin to displace conventional production at the margins. This question tracks whether such a product crosses from pilot/restaurant sales into mainstream grocery retail.

### Resolution criteria
Resolves **YES** if, before 31 December 2035:

- A ground or minced chicken product is available for purchase by general consumers in ≥100 retail grocery outlets (physical or major online grocery) in any G20 member country
- The product contains ≥10% cultivated chicken cells by raw/wet weight, as stated on product labeling, in verified company documentation, or confirmed by a credible third-party industry analyst
- "Cultivated chicken cells" means myogenic cells derived from live chickens and expanded in bioreactors — not plant-based ingredients, flavoring extracts, or cell-based seasonings
- The product is available for regular purchase (not a limited-run launch) — demonstrated by ≥3 consecutive months of retail availability

Resolves **NO** if no such product is available by that date.

### Fine print
- Products sold exclusively in restaurants, food service, or airline catering do not qualify — must be retail
- If labeling law in the jurisdiction does not require disclosure of cultivated content, resolution requires a credible independent verification (regulatory filing, third-party audit, or investigative industry report)
- Blended products where the cultivated fraction is derived from a non-chicken species do not qualify
- The 10% threshold is by raw (uncooked) weight of the cultivated cell component before mixing — not protein weight or dry weight

### Resolution date
31 December 2035

## Question 2 — Cultivated chicken: consumer taste equivalence to conventional ground chicken

### Title
Will a commercially available cultivated or cultivated-blend ground chicken product pass a pre-registered blinded taste equivalence test by 2035?

### Background
Consumer acceptance is a prerequisite for market adoption regardless of cost. A cultivated ground chicken product must be indistinguishable from (or preferred over) conventional ground chicken to achieve mainstream uptake. Laboratory prototypes have been evaluated informally by chefs, but rigorous blinded equivalence testing on a commercial product has not yet been published. This question tracks the publication of a credible third-party taste equivalence study on a product that is simultaneously commercially available — marking the moment where both production viability and consumer acceptance have been independently validated for the same product.

### Resolution criteria
Resolves **YES** if, before 31 December 2035, a peer-reviewed or pre-registered study is published demonstrating:

- A commercially available ground chicken product containing ≥10% cultivated chicken cells (same standard as Question 1) is assessed in a blinded taste test
- ≥100 participants who self-identify as regular chicken consumers (eating ground/minced chicken ≥ twice per month)
- Using a triangle discrimination test: the share of participants correctly identifying the cultivated/blend product as different from conventional ground chicken is ≤40% (not significantly above the 33% chance rate at p < 0.10)  
  *OR* using a paired preference test: ≥50% of participants rate the cultivated/blend product as "equivalent to or better than" conventional ground chicken overall
- The test is conducted by a third party independent of the product's manufacturer
- Cooking method is standardized and described (e.g., "pan-fried at 180°C for 4 minutes each side, no added seasoning")
- The product used in the test is the same commercially available product, purchased through normal channels

Resolves **NO** otherwise.

### Fine print
- Self-reported taste surveys published by cultivated meat companies without third-party blinding do not qualify
- Sensory tests conducted on laboratory prototypes (not the commercial product as sold) do not qualify
- A product failing a triangle test (i.e., participants *can* reliably detect a difference) but passing a preference test (they prefer or rate equally) resolves YES — the preference threshold is sufficient
- If the product is available in multiple cooking forms (burger, meatball, raw patty), the test must specify which form was evaluated; resolution applies to the specific form tested

### Resolution date
31 December 2035

## Question 3 — Precision fermentation egg white protein: price parity with conventional

### Title
Will precision fermentation-derived egg white protein (ovalbumin, ≥80% protein) be commercially available at ≤120% of the conventional dried egg white commodity price, in quantities ≥1 tonne, by 2033?

### Background
Precision fermentation (PF) uses engineered microorganisms to produce specific animal proteins. Ovalbumin — the dominant protein in egg whites (~54% of total egg white protein by weight) — has been produced via PF by companies including Every Company (formerly Clara Foods, US). Replacing conventional egg whites with PF-derived ovalbumin could reduce demand for egg production, with direct welfare benefits for laying hens and eliminate the routine culling of approximately 7 billion male chicks annually. Dried egg white protein (spray-dried, ≥80% protein content) is a well-established food ingredient with transparent commodity pricing. The "120% threshold" acknowledges that early commercial PF products typically carry a modest premium over conventional equivalents; this question asks whether the gap has narrowed to within 20%, indicating meaningful commercial competitiveness.

**Reference benchmark**: USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS) Egg Products Report, spray-dried egg white, average $/kg over the 12 months preceding the resolution date. If this series is discontinued, the FAO or FOEX equivalent may be substituted.

### Resolution criteria
Resolves **YES** if, before 31 December 2033:

- At least one company offers a food-grade PF-derived ovalbumin product (≥80% protein content by weight, spray-dried or equivalent dry form) for commercial purchase by food manufacturers
- The product is available in quantities ≥1 tonne per order (not research/sample quantities)
- The list price for ≥1 tonne orders is ≤120% of the USDA AMS Egg Products Report price for spray-dried conventional egg white averaged over the preceding 12 months
- Verified by: company price sheets corroborated by one independent source (distributor catalog, industry analyst report, or trade press)

Resolves **NO** otherwise.

### Fine print
- Products sold only for pharmaceutical/cosmetic applications (at pharma-grade specifications and margins) do not qualify — must be positioned for food manufacturing use
- If the product is not publicly listed at a stated price, verified wholesale quotes received by ≥3 independent food manufacturers constitute resolution
- The "120%" threshold is assessed at the time of resolution; if the USDA benchmark has high short-term volatility, a 12-month rolling average is used

### Resolution date
31 December 2033

## Question 4 — Cultivated shrimp (minced/processed form): price-competitive commercial availability

### Title
Will commercially available cultivated shrimp in minced or processed form (≥50% cultivated cells by weight) be sold at ≤2× the wholesale price of equivalent conventional processed shrimp by 2033?

### Background
Cultivated shrimp — crustacean cells grown in bioreactors — offers large animal welfare gains if successful at scale. Hundreds of billions of shrimp are farmed annually under conditions that include severe overcrowding, routine de-eyestalling of female broodstock (removal of the eyestalk to trigger reproduction), use of antibiotics, and extremely high mortality rates. Shrimp farming also drives mangrove deforestation across Southeast Asia and South America. The near-term commercially achievable form of cultivated shrimp is unlikely to be whole-tail (see Question 5) — the organized muscle fiber structure and exoskeletal snap of conventional shrimp are difficult to replicate in culture without complex scaffolding. Instead, early products will be minced or processed: shrimp mass suitable for use in dumplings, shrimp balls (popular in East Asian cuisine), shrimp paste, and other applications where whole-tail presentation is not required. The markets for these forms are large — shrimp paste alone is a staple ingredient across Southeast and East Asia.

**Reference benchmark**: FOB wholesale price for block-frozen minced shrimp (Penaeus vannamei or equivalent, standard food-service grade) from major producing countries (Thailand, Vietnam, Ecuador, India), averaged over the 12 months preceding resolution. Current indicative range: $5–10/kg. Source: URNER BARRY, FAO FishStat, or equivalent commodity pricing service.

### Resolution criteria
Resolves **YES** if, before 31 December 2033:

- A commercially available product is confirmed to contain ≥50% cultivated shrimp cells by raw wet weight (cells derived from crustacean species in the genus *Penaeus* or equivalent edible shrimp species, produced via cell culture without live shrimp slaughter)
- The product is in minced, processed, or paste form — NOT a whole discrete tail (see Question 5 for that milestone)
- Available for regular commercial purchase (food service or retail) in ≥1 country for ≥3 consecutive months
- List price for bulk orders (≥100kg) is ≤2× the FOB benchmark for equivalent-grade conventional minced shrimp averaged over the preceding 12 months
- Confirmed by company disclosure, regulatory approval documentation, or third-party industry reporting

Resolves **NO** otherwise.

### Fine print
- Products sold only as one-off restaurant promotions or at clearly luxury/novelty pricing without a standard commercial price list do not qualify
- A blend of ≥50% cultivated and ≤50% conventional shrimp qualifies (the cultivated fraction must be ≥50%)
- Plant-based or mycoprotein-based products are excluded even if they incorporate cultivated shrimp flavoring compounds
- The price comparison uses the cultivated product's bulk (food-service) price, not a single-serve retail premium — to avoid comparing wholesale conventional against artisanal retail cultivated

### Resolution date
31 December 2033

## Question 5 — Cultivated shrimp (whole tail): commercially available at competitive price

### Title
Will a commercially available whole-muscle cultivated shrimp tail (discrete tail morphology, ≥80% cultivated cells, no conventional shrimp casing) be sold at ≤3× the wholesale price of equivalent-size farmed shrimp in any market by 2038?

### Background
Whole-tail shrimp — the form familiar to consumers in Western and East Asian premium markets (cocktail shrimp, peel-and-eat, tempura whole shrimp) — presents a substantially greater technical challenge than minced/processed forms (Question 4). The characteristic texture of a whole shrimp tail comes from (1) highly organized parallel myofibril bundles running in abdominal segments, (2) connective tissue providing structural integrity, and (3) the hard chitin exoskeleton providing the external "snap." Replicating this in culture requires either structured scaffolding (allowing cells to organize into aligned fibers during growth) or advanced biofabrication approaches. A product that passes visual and textural inspection as a "shrimp tail" — without a conventional shrimp shell or substrate — represents a substantially harder milestone than minced forms. This question is accordingly set with a later resolution date and a looser price ratio (3× rather than 2×).

**Reference benchmark**: FOB wholesale price for headless, shell-on farmed shrimp, 16/20 count per pound (Penaeus vannamei or equivalent, approximately 22–28g per piece), from major producing countries. Averaged over 12 months preceding resolution. Source: URNER BARRY, FAO, or equivalent.

### Resolution criteria
Resolves **YES** if, before 31 December 2038:

- A commercially available product consists of discrete pieces that:
  - Have shrimp-tail morphology (elongated, segmented, visually recognizable as a shrimp-like tail piece)
  - Contain ≥80% cultivated crustacean cells by raw wet weight
  - Do not use a conventional shrimp exoskeleton or shell as an external casing or scaffold (a food-grade synthetic coating that mimics shrimp texture is acceptable)
- The product is available for regular commercial purchase in ≥1 country for ≥3 consecutive months
- List price for ≥10kg bulk orders is ≤3× the FOB benchmark for equivalent-size conventional farmed shrimp averaged over the preceding 12 months
- Confirmed by company disclosure, independent food industry reporting, or regulatory documentation

Resolves **NO** otherwise.

### Fine print
- Products where a conventional shrimp shell is filled with cultivated shrimp mass do not qualify — it must be a freestanding cultivated piece
- The "3× price" threshold acknowledges that whole-tail cultivated shrimp will carry a substantial premium at first commercial launch; the question asks whether a commercially viable price has been achieved, not price parity
- A product available only in a single restaurant or tasting-menu context (not purchasable through a standard commercial channel) does not qualify
- Verification that the product contains ≥80% cultivated cells must come from a source independent of the manufacturer (regulatory filing, third-party audit, academic analysis, or investigative trade reporting)

### Resolution date
31 December 2038
